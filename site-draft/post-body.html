Why screenshot and raw-text diffs are noisy on competitor pages

Pricing and legal pages look static, but their HTML is not. Every request can carry a new CSRF token, a "last updated 3 minutes ago" label, a rotating testimonial, a cookie banner, an A/B-tested headline or a cache-busting query string. A plain screenshot or raw-text diff reports all of that as a change. Some services add filtering on top (Visualping, for example, attaches an AI summary and an important-or-not flag to every change), but a page-level alert still leaves you to work out what moved. After a week of false alarms people stop reading the alerts, and the one real price increase goes unnoticed.

What you actually want to know is narrower: did a plan's price, currency or billing period change, was a plan added or removed, which section of the Terms changed and how, which roles opened or closed, which product pages appeared? That needs a comparison that understands the page type.

Two noise filters matter most in practice. First, a change is confirmed with a second read before it is reported: in our tests Slack's pricing page served two variants within seconds, and the confirmation read filtered it out. Second, the Actor remembers recent versions, so a page that alternates between two versions across runs alerts once and then only as a low-severity "switched back" item. Politeness rules we follow

  • Public, no-login pages only. No cookies or sessions, no CAPTCHA solving, no proxies to get around blocks.
  • robots.txt (RFC 9309) is checked for every page and redirect; disallowed pages are skipped and reported. Crawl-delay is honoured.
  • Honest user agent (FairqueryChangeTracker/1.0 with a contact address), one request at a time per host, at least 1.5 s apart, conditional requests (ETag / If-Modified-Since).
  • Plain HTTP first. A headless browser is opt-in, only for JavaScript-rendered pricing pages, and still respects robots.txt.
  • A 403 or 429 stops all requests to that host for the rest of the run.
